Modelled Investigation · writing up results

Turn a result into a finding that earns marks

These are made-up results from Ysgol Cwm Coleg's overdue-books research — the kind of thing Microsoft Forms would generate automatically from your questionnaire responses. A result on its own doesn't earn marks. A finding does three things: states what the data actually shows, says what that reveals needs improving, and says what it means for the system you're going to build.

Same fictional library scenario as the rest of the Investigation resources. Use the sentence stem below as a starting point — drop it once your own writing does the same three jobs without it.

Sentence stem
We found [the actual data]. This suggests [what's not working / what needs improving], which means my system needs to [what this implies for the solution].
MS Forms tip
Open your form's Responses tab — Forms builds a chart per question automatically. Screenshot the chart straight into your write-up rather than retyping the numbers, then write your finding underneath it.
Q1Questionnaire · all students

"How do you currently find out that a book you've borrowed is overdue?"

Weak

41% of students said they find out a book is overdue when they return to the desk.

Just restates one number from the chart. No problem named, no link to the solution.
Strong

We found that 41% of students only discover a book is overdue when they return to the desk, and a further 9% never find out until a letter is sent home. This suggests the current paper-slip system is failing to reach half of borrowers before their loan is significantly overdue, which means my system needs to notify borrowers directly and immediately — for example by email — rather than relying on a slip that depends on the weekly round happening at all.

Q2Questionnaire · all students

"Roughly how long does it take from a book becoming overdue to you finding out?"

Weak

49% of students said it takes about a week to find out, and 28% said more than a week.

Two numbers copied out with "and" between them — still just describing the chart.
Strong

We found that 77% of students wait a week or more before finding out a book is overdue at all. This is a significant delay for a process that's meant to run weekly, and it means fines and follow-up letters are being triggered later than they should be — so my system needs to flag an overdue loan and alert the borrower on the day it becomes overdue, not whenever the next manual round happens to catch it.

Q3Interview · library assistant

"Can you walk me through what the overdue round actually involves?"

"Cross-checking and writing the slips can take over three hours some Fridays. And if I'm not in that day, it just doesn't happen that week — it waits until whoever's next on the desk gets to it."Library assistant, Cwm Bran campus
Weak

The library assistant said the overdue round takes three hours and depends on who's on the desk.

Reports what was said, but doesn't say why it's a problem or what it means for the build.
Strong

We found that the overdue round takes up to three hours of staff time a week and relies entirely on a specific member of staff being on the desk that Friday — if they're absent, the whole round is skipped rather than covered. This shows the current process has no resilience to staff absence, which means my system needs to run the overdue check automatically rather than depending on a person remembering to do it.

Q4Observation · library records

Where are overdue reminders currently recorded, before they're sent out?

100%of overdue records exist only in the paper loan ledger — no digital copy or backup exists anywhere else.
Weak

Overdue records are only kept in the paper ledger.

True, but doesn't explain the risk or connect it to a system requirement.
Strong

We found that every overdue record exists only in a single paper ledger, with no digital copy or backup. This is a single point of failure — if the ledger is lost, damaged, or simply left at the wrong campus, that week's overdue data is gone completely — which means my system needs to store loan and overdue data digitally, ideally with the data accessible from all three campuses rather than tied to one physical book.

Before you write your own results section, check each finding:

  • States the actual data or evidence, not a vague impression of it
  • Names specifically what this shows isn't working, or needs improving
  • Says what that means for the system you're going to design and build
