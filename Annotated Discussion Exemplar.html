What a Band 3 Discussion actually looks like

A modelled extract, written to demonstrate the four things the Discussion band descriptor is looking for — with each part annotated against the exact bullet it's evidencing.

This is a composite example written for teaching, not a real student's submission. The "library" scenario is the same one used in the Lesson 1 slides, so you can follow the same thread across both.

Discussion — Band 3 (4–5 marks)

A substantial problem giving full scope · broad aims in technical vocabulary · limitations described in technical detail · feedback fully considered and used to refine your understanding.

01

Background

evidences → "a substantial problem giving full scope"

College Library operates mainly as a physical, walk-in service. Students and staff must visit the Learning Resource Centre in person to check whether an item is available, to borrow it, or to return it. The library serves roughly 600 students and 40 staff across three campuses1, and day-to-day running is the responsibility of the Librarian and two library assistants2, who also handle enquiries, shelving and stock ordering from the same desk.

1Real numbers, not "lots of students." Scale like this is what makes a problem substantial — not just calling it that.
2Named roles, not "staff." The examiner needs to see exactly who is affected and who currently does the work.
02

The current system

evidences → "limitations described in technical detail"

This creates several limitations. There is no searchable online catalogue3, so a student cannot check whether a book is on the shelf, or which campus holds it, before travelling there. Overdue reminders are generated manually: a library assistant cross-checks a paper ledger against return dates once a week, by hand — a process that currently takes around three hours and is often delayed when staffing is tight4. Loans, renewals and reservations can only be processed in person at the desk5, so a student based at a different campus from where a book is held has no way to reserve it remotely.

3A technical fact about the system, not an opinion — that's the line between a limitation and a complaint.
4The hardest-working sentence in the whole extract: a named process, a frequency, a duration. "It's slow" scores nothing here — "three hours, once a week, by hand" is technical detail.
5Every limitation is tied to a consequence for a specific kind of user, not left as a standalone fact.
03

The proposed system

evidences → "broad aims in technical vocabulary" (and a full-scope judgement)

The proposed system will provide a searchable online catalogue accessible from any campus6, allow students to reserve and renew items remotely6, and automatically generate overdue notifications by email6 rather than by hand. It will not replace in-person borrowing7 — the physical collection stays as it is — and it will not manage inter-library loans with other institutions7, which is out of scope for the time available.

Objectives:

  1. Students can search real-time item availability across all three campuses.
  2. A reservation request is logged and confirmed within one working day.
  3. Overdue notices are generated automatically, without staff intervention8.
6"Searchable," "remotely," "automatically generate" — precise technical vocabulary, not vague ambition like "make it easier to use."
7Stating what the system will not do is a full-scope judgement — it shows you drew the boundary deliberately, not that you ran out of ideas.
8Each objective is measurable. "Within one working day" can be tested and marked met or not met — "improve the library" can't be.
04

Feedback

evidences → "feedback fully considered and used to refine your understanding"

I discussed this proposal with the Librarian, who confirmed the three-hour weekly reminder process was accurate, but pointed out that some older students without regular email access still rely on posted letters9. As a result, I revised Objective 3 to offer both an email notice and an opt-in printed letter10, rather than assuming email would reach every student. A peer reviewer also asked how reservations would be prioritised if two students requested the same item at once; I addressed this by adding a first-come, first-served rule10 to the specification.

9Feedback is attributed to a named person, and stated as a specific point — not "they said it was good."
10The pattern to copy: who said it → what they said → what changed because of it. That third step is what "used to refine your understanding" means — a comment you didn't act on isn't evidence.
