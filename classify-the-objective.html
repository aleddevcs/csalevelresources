Practical, general, or something else? Classify each statement

Your working specification needs five different kinds of statement: practical objectives, general objectives, and the performance, security and quality checklists. They can look similar at a glance — especially practical vs. general. Read each statement below (all drafted for Ysgol Cwm Coleg's library system, the same scenario you've used throughout the Investigation) and classify which part of the specification it belongs to.

These are model statements for the fictional library system — use them to practise spotting the difference, then apply the same thinking to your own system's objectives and checklists.

PRPractical

Specific, measurable, actionable — concrete enough to plan and test against.

GEGeneral

High-level and abstract — the broad vision, no feature-level detail.

PFPerformance

Metrics and criteria, how they're measured, how issues get fixed.

SESecurity

Who's allowed to access or change what, and how that's enforced and checked.

QUQuality

Works correctly, looks consistent, reads well as code, gets properly checked.
